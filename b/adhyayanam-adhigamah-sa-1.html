adhyayanam – adhigamaḥ

Adhyayana and Adhigama

संस्कृतम् · Sanskrit · Indian Knowledge Systems & Educational Psychology · 597 pp. · Dr. Mantha Srinivasu
Google PlayLibrary

How does a learner actually come to know? This first volume of the Indian Knowledge Tradition – Educational Psychology Series reconstructs the theories of learning embedded in India's intellectual traditions and sets them in a rigorous, criterion-based comparison with Western educational psychology.

The book moves from the Vedic–Upaniṣadic model of śravaṇa, manana and nididhyāsana to pramāṇa-śāstra as a theory of how knowledge is generated, and to the doctrine of saṃskāra and vāsanā as an Indian account of retention and transfer. Separate chapters examine the Buddhist contribution of transformative learning, the Jaina stages of mati-jñāna and śruta-jñāna, and the theory of language acquisition in Pāṇini, Bhartṛhari and the sphoṭa doctrine, before turning to the gurukula as a carefully designed learning environment.

A systematic review of Western learning theories is followed by an honest account of convergences, divergences and points of incommensurability, an integrated model for the Indian classroom, and an agenda for future research. Appendices, a Sanskrit–English glossary, an index of primary texts, a bibliography and a full index make the volume a working reference for teachers, teacher-educators, researchers and students of Education, Sanskrit and Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS), in the spirit of NEP 2020.

Keywords: Indian Knowledge Systems, IKS, learning theory, adhigama, educational psychology, Sanskrit, Upanishad, pramana, samskara, gurukula, Panini, NEP 2020, teacher education
