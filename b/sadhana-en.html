Every prescription about teaching contains a psychological claim; the only question is whether it is stated or smuggled. Sādhana — "the means" — sets out the psychology embedded in the Indian traditions and holds it to a demanding standard: does it specify something a teacher could do and a researcher could check?

In twenty-six chapters, written in English, this volume brings together the whole field of educational psychology. It covers learning (śravaṇa, manana, nididhyāsana), pramāṇa as a theory of learning, memory (smṛti, saṃskāra, vāsanā), attention (dhāraṇā, dhyāna, ekāgratā), language and multilingual learning, intelligence and individual difference, personality, motivation, emotion, creativity (pratibhā, vyutpatti, abhyāsa), human development, character, habit and self-regulation, and play and childhood (krīḍā and līlā). It then turns to the teacher, the learning environment (gurukula and saṅgha), curriculum, skill and vocational learning (śilpa and śreṇī), guidance and mental health, assessment, and difference, disability and inclusion — including the tradition's own exclusions.

It concludes with a frank analysis of convergence, divergence and incommensurability, and with an integrated framework and a research programme of twelve testable propositions. It is the companion to Darśana and a one-volume English synthesis of the ten-volume Sanskrit series, ideal for B.Ed., M.Ed., Ph.D. and IKS courses.

Keywords: educational psychology, Indian psychology, sadhana, learning, memory, attention, motivation, personality, creativity, inclusion, teacher education, Indian Knowledge Systems, IKS, B.Ed, M.Ed
