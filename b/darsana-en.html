Darśana

Darśana

दर्शनम्

What is knowledge, that a school should transmit it? What is a person, that a person can be formed? What is education finally for? Darśana — "seeing", a way of viewing reality — asks the philosophical questions of education and answers them from the Indian traditions, in careful comparison with Western philosophies of education.

In twenty-six chapters the book moves through epistemology (vidyā and avidyā, the two knowledges, pramāṇa as a theory of valid knowing, truth and error, śabda and the limits of authority), metaphysics (what is real, the self and the learner, freedom and agency, consciousness) and value (dharma, the puruṣārthas as the aims of education, śreyas and preyas, rasa and the aesthetic, the moral content of schooling). It then addresses hermeneutics and dialogue — how to read an authoritative text, language and meaning, vāda as an ethics of argument, and anekāntavāda as plurality without relativism — before turning to adhikāra and justice and the guru–śiṣya relationship.

After a review of Western philosophies of education and a chapter on comparison and commensurability, it concludes by setting out an Indian philosophy of education. Written in clear English with a glossary of Sanskrit, Prakrit and Pali terms, an index of primary texts, a bibliography and an index, it is the companion to Sādhana and an ideal text for courses on philosophy of education and Indian Knowledge Systems.
