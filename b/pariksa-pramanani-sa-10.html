Parīkṣā and Pramāṇa

संस्कृतम् · Sanskrit · Indian Knowledge Systems & Educational Psychology · 946 pp. · Dr. Mantha Srinivasu
Google PlayLibrary

Our examination culture runs without a theory of evaluation. The tenth and concluding volume of the Sanskrit series recovers India's own traditions of assessment, debate and certification, and sets them against Western educational measurement.

The book treats parīkṣā as an epistemological category; śāstrārtha and vāda as performance-based assessment; the Vimāna-sthāna of the Caraka-saṃhitā as a classical assessment rubric; the certification of competence (adhikāra) and the conferring of honours; and the assessment of skill in recitation and reading. A structured review of Western educational measurement and of taxonomies of learning outcomes follows.

After comparison and synthesis, the book proposes reforms of assessment on an Indian foundation and closes with directions for research. As the largest volume of the series, with extensive appendices, a glossary, an index of primary texts, a bibliography and an index, it is an indispensable resource for examination bodies, teachers, curriculum designers and researchers in evaluation and Indian Knowledge Systems.

Keywords: assessment, evaluation, examination reform, pariksha, shastrartha, debate, Charaka Samhita, certification, educational measurement, learning outcomes, IKS, Sanskrit
